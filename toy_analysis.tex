\section{Toy Linear Verification of Closed-Form Finetuning Solutions}
\label{sec:toy}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We construct paired ``image'' and ``text'' features from a shared low-rank latent, a standard toy protocol in the contrastive learning literature. Let
\(
\mathbf X \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n},\;
\mathbf X_T \in \mathbb{R}^{d_T \times n}
\)
be generated as
\(
\mathbf X = \mathbf U_I \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf s)\,\mathbf G + \sigma_I \varepsilon_I,\;
\mathbf X_T = \mathbf U_T \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf s)\,\mathbf G + \sigma_T \varepsilon_T,
\)
where \(\mathbf U_I \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r},\mathbf U_T \in \mathbb{R}^{d_T \times r}\) have orthonormal columns, \(\mathbf s\) decays geometrically (anisotropic spectrum), and \(\mathbf G \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}\) is standard normal. We freeze a random text head \(\mathbf W_T^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times d_T}\) and initialize an image head \(\mathbf W_I^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times d}\). The contrastive surrogate target is
\(
\mathbf Y_{\mathrm{FT}} = \mathbf W_T^0 \mathbf X_T \mathbf H
\),
with \(\mathbf H = \mathbf I - \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf 1 \mathbf 1^\top\)
(the usual centering matrix). We denote
\(
\mathbf C = \mathbf X \mathbf X^\top,\;
\mathcal P = \mathbf X(\mathbf X^\top \mathbf X)^{\dagger}\mathbf X^\top
\)
(the data-space projector), and \(\mathbf C^{\dagger}\) the MP pseudoinverse.

\paragraph{Closed forms (re-stated).}
With these definitions, the solutions in Sec.~\ref{sec:FT} specialize to:
\begin{align*}
\mathbf W_{\mathrm{FT}}
&= \mathbf W_I^0 (\mathbf I - \mathcal P) + \mathbf Y_{\mathrm{FT}}\mathbf X^\top \mathbf C^{\dagger},\\
\mathbf W_{L_2}
&= \big(\mathbf Y_{\mathrm{FT}}\mathbf X^\top + \lambda\,\mathbf W_I^0\big)\big(\mathbf C + \lambda \mathbf I\big)^{-1},\\
\mathbf W_{\mathrm{SD}}
&= \mathbf W_I^0\!\left(\mathbf I - \tfrac{1}{1+\lambda}\mathcal P\right) + \tfrac{1}{1+\lambda}\,\mathbf Y_{\mathrm{FT}}\mathbf X^\top \mathbf C^{\dagger}.
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Optimizers.}
We consider (i) \emph{plain gradient descent} (GD) on each quadratic objective with a step size \(\gamma\) chosen from the Lipschitz constant of the gradient, and (ii) a \emph{right-preconditioned} GD that multiplies the gradient by \(\mathbf C^{\dagger}\) (or \(((1+\lambda)\mathbf C)^{\dagger}\) for SD). The preconditioned updates are:
\[
\text{FT:}\quad
\mathbf W \leftarrow \mathbf W - \big(\mathbf W\mathbf C - \mathbf Y_{\mathrm{FT}}\mathbf X^\top\big)\mathbf C^{\dagger},
\qquad
\text{SD:}\quad
\mathbf W \leftarrow \mathbf W - \big((1+\lambda)\mathbf W\mathbf C - \mathbf Y_{\mathrm{FT}}\mathbf X^\top - \lambda \mathbf W_I^0 \mathbf C\big)\frac{\mathbf C^{\dagger}}{1+\lambda}.
\]
For \(\gamma=1\), these updates reach \(\mathbf W_{\mathrm{FT}}\) and \(\mathbf W_{\mathrm{SD}}\) in a \emph{single} step (algebraically exact), providing a sharp numerical verification of the closed forms.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report: (i) relative Frobenius error between the numerical iterate and the closed form;
(ii) an orthogonal-preservation diagnostic
\(\|(\mathbf W-\mathbf W_I^0)(\mathbf I-\mathcal P)\|_F/\|\mathbf W_I^0(\mathbf I-\mathcal P)\|_F\)
(which should be zero for the closed forms);
and (iii) spectral checks for L2-SP and SD along eigenpairs of \(\mathbf C\) in \(\mathrm{range}(\mathcal P)\).

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:toy-linear} shows means over five random seeds. With plain GD (1500 steps), $L_2$ reaches its closed form to within $1.1\times 10^{-6}$ relative error, but FT and SD converge slowly in ill-conditioned directions ($0.85$ and $0.55$ mean errors). In contrast, the preconditioned GD achieves $\mathbf W_{\mathrm{FT}}$ and $\mathbf W_{\mathrm{SD}}$ in a \emph{single} step, with mean errors of $3.8\times 10^{-13}$ and $2.5\times 10^{-13}$, respectively. Orthogonal-preservation residuals are small ($\lesssim 3\%$), and spectral shrinkage matches theory to machine precision (mean discrepancy $\sim 10^{-14}$ for L2, $\sim 10^{-15}$ for SD, correlation $=1.0$).

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
The experiment confirms the closed-form solutions in three ways: (i) exact match via preconditioned GD, (ii) orthogonal-subspace preservation, and (iii) eigenvalue-dependent shrinkage. The apparent gap with plain GD is purely an optimizer effect in ill-conditioned problems.
